Measuring the Time-Varying Market Efficiency in the Prewar and Wartime Japanese Stock Market, 1924–1943

Abstract

This paper uses a new market-capitalization-weighted index to investigate whether efficiency in the Japanese stock market changed between 1924 and 1943. The estimates reveal substantial variation around major historical events and support a time-varying view of market efficiency. As wartime controls intensified and the prospect of wider war increased, market efficiency deteriorated.

Kenichi Hirayama and Akihiko Noda

Asia-Pacific Economic History Review, 65(1), 131–159 (2025)

An Alternative Estimation Method for Time-Varying Parameter Models

Abstract

This study develops a non-Bayesian, feasible generalized least squares approach for estimating time-varying parameters in multivariate vector autoregressions. The method offers an alternative to Bayesian estimation while limiting the pile-up problem that can arise in other estimation procedures. It can accommodate stochastic volatility, changing error covariances, and abrupt movements in model coefficients.

Mikio Ito, Akihiko Noda, and Tatsuma Wada

Econometrics, 10(2), 23 (2022)

Examining the Dynamic Asset Market Linkages under the COVID-19 Global Pandemic

Abstract

Using a generalized least squares-based time-varying vector autoregression, this study examines how cross-asset linkages and joint market efficiency changed during the COVID-19 pandemic. Efficiency varies substantially over time, while stronger connections among asset markets during the pandemic may have reduced arbitrage opportunities. The subsequent decline in efficiency is associated with the Bitcoin price boom beginning in late 2020.

Akihiko Noda

Economics Bulletin, 42(2), 653–661 (2022)

Time-Varying Comovement of Foreign Exchange Markets: A GLS-Based Time-Varying Model Approach

Abstract

This study investigates changes in long-run foreign-exchange market comovement. Exchange rates for Canada, Japan, and the United Kingdom against the US dollar are analyzed using a vector error-correction model with time-varying adjustment coefficients. Estimated comovement becomes stronger over the sample period, but its rate of increase slows, with notable turning points around 1995 and 2008.

Mikio Ito, Akihiko Noda, and Tatsuma Wada

Mathematics, 9(8), 849 (2021)

On the Evolution of Cryptocurrency Market Efficiency

Abstract

This paper evaluates how efficiency evolves in the Bitcoin and Ethereum markets using a generalized least squares-based time-varying model. Efficiency fluctuates over time, with Bitcoin generally exhibiting a higher degree of efficiency than Ethereum. Improvements in market liquidity are associated with market development, providing evidence consistent with the adaptive market hypothesis, particularly for the more established cryptocurrency.

Akihiko Noda

Applied Economics Letters, 28(6), 433–439 (2021)

The Futures Premium and Rice Market Efficiency in Prewar Japan

Abstract

This paper analyzes the relationship between spot prices and futures premia in the principal rice markets of prewar Japan. A time-varying econometric framework identifies periods of reduced efficiency and relates them to the structure of rice trading and differences between domestic and colonial rice. Government intervention did not, by itself, resolve these disruptions, whereas changes in the characteristics of colonial rice helped reduce market frictions.

Mikio Ito, Kiyotaka Maeda, and Akihiko Noda

Economic History Review, 71(3), 909–937 (2018)

Market Efficiency and Government Interventions in Prewar Japanese Rice Futures Markets

Abstract

Combining a time-varying vector autoregressive model with historical evidence, this paper studies government intervention in Tokyo and Osaka rice futures markets between 1881 and 1932. The two markets were generally close to efficient, but measures allowing delivery of rice imported from Taiwan and Korea often impaired efficiency. The adverse effects persisted while significant quality differences remained between domestic and colonial rice.

Mikio Ito, Kiyotaka Maeda, and Akihiko Noda

Financial History Review, 23(3), 325–346 (2016)

A Test of the Adaptive Market Hypothesis using a Time-Varying AR Model in Japan

Abstract

This study tests the adaptive market hypothesis in Japan using a time-varying autoregressive model for TOPIX and the Tokyo Stock Exchange's second-section market. Market efficiency changes over time in both segments, although the second-section market is less efficient for much of the sample. The results indicate that efficiency evolves in the more established market without necessarily following the same path in smaller-stock trading.

Akihiko Noda

Finance Research Letters, 17, 66–71 (2016)

The Evolution of Stock Market Efficiency in the US: A Non-Bayesian Time-Varying Model Approach

Abstract

This paper develops a non-Bayesian time-varying autoregressive measure of US stock-market efficiency and studies its evolution over a long historical period. Estimated efficiency exhibits fluctuations lasting several decades and falls during several important economic episodes. These findings indicate that market efficiency is not fixed and are partly consistent with an adaptive view of financial-market behavior.

Mikio Ito, Akihiko Noda, and Tatsuma Wada

Applied Economics, 48(7), 621–635 (2016)

International Stock Market Efficiency: A Non-Bayesian Time-Varying Model Approach

Abstract

A non-Bayesian time-varying vector autoregressive framework is applied to international equity markets in the G7 countries. The approach tracks changes in cross-market linkages and their joint degree of efficiency. Both measures evolve over time, and notable movements correspond to major events in the international financial system, suggesting that market integration and efficiency depend on historical conditions.

Mikio Ito, Akihiko Noda, and Tatsuma Wada

Applied Economics, 46(23), 2744–2754 (2014)

A Survey on the Estimation of CCAPMs via Moment Restrictions: The Case of Japan

Abstract

This survey reviews the estimation of the standard consumption-based capital asset pricing model through moment restrictions, focusing on empirical evidence from Japan. It introduces generalized method of moments estimation, discusses its limitations, and examines generalized empirical likelihood as an alternative with different higher-order bias properties. The paper considers implications for Japanese asset-pricing puzzles and outlines methodological questions for further study.

Akihiko Noda

Keio Economic Studies, 49, 69–91 (2013)

The GEL Estimates Resolve the Risk-free Rate Puzzle in Japan

Abstract

This study reexamines the risk-free rate puzzle in Japan using the consumption-based capital asset pricing model. Generalized empirical likelihood methods are employed to estimate the subjective discount factor and the coefficient of relative risk aversion. Comparing higher-order biases and asymptotic variances across estimation methods, the paper argues that the Japanese data do not exhibit the conventional risk-free rate puzzle.

Mikio Ito and Akihiko Noda

Applied Financial Economics, 22(5), 365–374 (2012)

Testing the “Catching up with the Joneses” Model with Consumption Externality: Some Evidence from Japan

Abstract

Using Japanese financial data, this paper tests Abel's 'catching up with the Joneses' model with consumption externalities. Model parameters are estimated using generalized empirical likelihood methods. The empirical evidence rejects the model for Japan, suggesting that the particular consumption-externality mechanism is not supported by the data analyzed.

Akihiko Noda

Economics Bulletin, 31(2), 1648–1658 (2011)

Measuring the Intertemporal Elasticity of Substitution for Consumption: Some Evidence from Japan

Abstract

This paper estimates the intertemporal elasticity of substitution in Japanese consumption under constant-relative-risk-aversion preferences. With quarterly consumption data, the continuous updating estimator produces estimates of approximately 0.2 to 0.5. Compared with standard two-step generalized method of moments estimation, the continuous updating approach addresses weak identification more effectively. Estimated elasticities also depend on the frequency of the consumption data.

Akihiko Noda and Shunsuke Sugiyama

Economics Bulletin, 30(1), 524–533 (2010)
